## Dated primary evidence and the cash-flow bridge

**Microsoft — demand monetization versus capacity costs.** The July 29 FY2026 release reports $331.8 billion annual revenue and $155.2 billion operating income; reported EPS of $17.95 includes $0.67 from OpenAI investment gains. Azure growth and contracted commitments are demand evidence, while investment returns and delivery capacity remain separate questions. [Microsoft FY2026 results](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast). Model higher electricity/network payments, capital expenditure and slower deployment against utilization, revenue realization and depreciation. Our annual CFO-minus-cash-PPE proxy is $66.99 billion; it excludes finance-lease additions. A 100bp group operating-margin change is arithmetically $3.32 billion EBIT at unchanged sales, not an estimated effect of an election. Microsoft's January 13 community commitments provide evidence that allocation of infrastructure costs is an operating issue; they are company commitments, not a universal regulatory rule. [Community-first infrastructure commitments](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2026/01/13/community-first-ai-infrastructure/).

**Vertiv — orders, physical delivery and price discipline.** The July 29 release reports second-quarter sales of $3.274 billion, up 24%, including 18% organic growth; adjusted operating margin was 22.6%. Management raised full-year guidance. [Vertiv Q2 results and guidance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1674101/000162828026050323/q22026exhibit991vrt07292026.htm). Translate project delays into revenue timing, backlog conversion, factory absorption and inventory/receivables rather than automatically writing off orders. Our matching-period GAAP operating margin is 18.94% and cash FCF proxy $2.93 billion. These do not equal management's adjusted quarterly measures. An 11.73% common-window price decline alongside strong reported growth is a question about expectations and delivery, not sufficient evidence of mispricing. The trailing GAAP P/E proxy of 63.47x warrants an explicit margin/volume downside case before considering a long allocation.

**AEP — who funds new load.** On July 30 AEP raised 2026 operating-EPS guidance to $6.25–$6.55 and discussed 69 GW of committed new load through 2030. [AEP Q2 release](https://www.aep.com/news/stories/view/12135/). The investment mechanism depends on customer commitments, collateral, minimum bills, network capital spending, financing and eventual rate recovery. Large headline load commitments are not realized annual sales. [AEP Ohio data-center tariff explanation](https://www.aepohio.com/company/about/rates/data-center-tariff/). Higher approved utility investment can coexist with worse near-term shareholder FCF; allocating more connection risk to the customer can protect existing ratepayers and utility economics but raise the customer's hurdle rate. The cached companyfacts response stops in March despite a newer filing: **all selected current TTM and balance-sheet figures and valuation proxies are withheld**. The July guidance is a dated management statement, not a replacement for a reconstructed quarterly statement.

**Exxon Mobil — energy protection with normalization risk.** July 31 results report Q2 earnings of $14.5 billion and CFO of $23.6 billion. [Exxon Q2 release](https://investor.exxonmobil.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0002115436-26-000006/livef8k2q26991.htm). Model upstream realizations, production, refining margins, inventory/working capital, taxes and buyback capacity separately; a supply disruption can benefit some margins while harming physical throughput. Our June-ended TTM cash FCF proxy is $30.55 billion, trailing P/E 20.52x and cash FCF yield 4.66%. These are current-period arithmetic proxies, not sustainable midcycle valuation. Legacy CIK34088 ends in March; successor CIK2115436 provides June facts. Combining them is explicitly justified by the one-for-one July 1 holding-company reorganization, with per-fact entity provenance retained. [Reorganization and June report](https://investor.exxonmobil.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000034088-26-000093/xom-20260630.htm).

**Walmart — household protection can be costly to supply.** The August 20 release reports $187.9 billion quarterly revenue, 5.9% growth, and adjusted EPS of $0.81; US comparable sales increased 2.6%. [Walmart fiscal Q2 results](https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-26-000145/earningsreleasefy27q2.htm). Stress trade-down volume and mix against import costs, cost pass-through, wage expense, logistics, advertising income and inventory financing. Affordability pressure can raise traffic while reducing merchandise profitability. Our July-ended operating margin is 4.43%; 100bp of sales is $7.28 billion EBIT. A 38.82x trailing P/E and 1.59% cash FCF yield proxy make entry valuation a separate question from defensive demand. No tariff-refund revenue or relief is assumed in the model without company-level evidence.

## Controls and portfolio use

All statement values are USD unless the CSV unit states shares, USD/shares, ratio or multiple. TTM flows require identical-tag annual plus current fiscal YTD minus matching prior YTD. EPS arithmetic is approximate when diluted share counts change; trailing multiples require further normalization for one-offs, splits and the share basis. Market-cap proxies multiply the September 4 raw close by the latest reported common-share count, which is not a current diluted share count.

Selected TTM/instant values older than 180 days, historical annual values older than 460 days, or snapshot values earlier than a known newer annual/quarterly filing are withheld. Missing standardized debt tags do not mean zero debt. Bank FCF is omitted. AEP has 11 stale snapshot values; Exxon debt has two, JPM cash one, UNH current debt one. Raw responses retain every historical observation and retrieval timestamp. Data are current vendor histories, not archived point-in-time backtests.

The appropriate 12–24 month action is a prioritized underwriting list: distinguish regulated cost recovery from merchant power pricing; compare current energy cash generation with normalization downside before using producers as portfolio protection; test housing incentive relief against weaker employment; and separate healthcare payer/provider policy incidence. Size any resulting long allocation from scenario cash flows and valuation downside, with sector and issuer constraints. The two-month return study is a screening input, not a buy/sell ranking, causal estimate or claim about crowded ownership.
