# Election and public-issue evidence for the midterm investment report

**Information cutoff: 8 September 2026, 15:07 UTC.** This memo accompanies `config/midterm_public_evidence.json`. It contains primary-source observations and explicitly labeled investment inferences. Publications were retrieved after the cutoff but screened for pre-cutoff publication or document vintage. It is a selected evidence packet, not a census of every poll or policy announcement.

The strongest supported development is **rising opposition to data-center costs, alongside a substantial rise in concern about gasoline**. The evidence does not support describing every affordability issue as newly accelerating. Healthcare and housing concerns were already high; national inflation priority eased in one comparable survey. AI anxiety is becoming more pronounced among young adults, but the evidence has not established that it is changing their turnout or votes.

## 1. Start with the offices voters can actually change

The House has 435 voting seats with two-year terms. Its district representation differs fundamentally from the Senate's state representation; a national generic ballot cannot be translated mechanically into either chamber's control. The Senate Press Gallery's explicitly dated July 14 lineup was 53 Republicans, 45 Democrats and two independents caucusing with Democrats. Its 2026 election list comprises 13 Democratic and 22 Republican seats, including special-election seats. A change in national sentiment therefore encounters an uneven set of states and incumbencies. [House institutional explanation](https://www.house.gov/the-house-explained), [Senate dated lineup](https://www.dailypress.senate.gov/on-the-floor/senate-facts/).

NCSL's June 25 inventory identifies 6,139 legislative seats across 46 states, with elections in 88 of 99 chambers. NGA's August 3 release identifies 39 gubernatorial contests including territories. These are separate sources of policy change: governor turnover need not bring legislative control, and party-count control can differ from the coalition organizing a chamber. [NCSL election inventory](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/ncsl-state-elections-2026), [NGA election context](https://www.nga.org/news/press-releases/highlights-from-the-2026-nga-summer-meeting/).

The FEC calendar sets the general election for November 3. Its May 18 vintage places New Hampshire's primary on September 8, Rhode Island's on September 9 and Delaware's on September 15; Louisiana's House process can extend to December 12. The frozen analysis must not use later primary results. Dates require state-office verification before trading around a particular resolution. [FEC dated calendar](https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/2026pdates.pdf).

| Institution | What the election analysis should distinguish | Investment relevance |
|---|---|---|
| House | Control, majority margin, district exposure, committee leadership and coalition cohesion | Tax/spending negotiations, oversight and the feasibility of specific legislation |
| Senate | Which states and seats change, governing caucus and confirmation leverage | Legislative passage and nominations have different procedural thresholds |
| Governor plus state chambers | Unified/divided control, veto capacity and implementation deadlines | State tax incentives, health administration, land-use legislation and agency direction |
| Utility commission, state agencies and local government | Appointment rules, existing orders, local approval and project status | Actual cost allocation and permit execution may move without a congressional majority change |
| Federal executive and Fed | Ordinary continuity of the presidential term; independent monetary-policy decisions | An opposition congressional gain is not a presidential transition or an automatic rate cut |

For most Senate legislation, simple-majority passage and three-fifths cloture are different hurdles; the vice president can break a tie. Reconciliation and other exceptions require proposal-specific analysis. Accordingly, a Kalshi chamber-control probability is an input to a policy scenario, not the probability that a campaign promise becomes law. [Senate voting procedures](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/voting.htm).

**Minimum viable election research:** use the full market catalogue for coverage, but concentrate document matching on contested federal/state offices with a plausible issue-to-authority link. Wisconsin is a documented state case below; Pennsylvania, Maine, Colorado and Ohio provide actual policy cases. Do not pretend this packet estimates all 6,139 legislative races. Treat national, state and office-specific probabilities as related marginals, not independent events.

**Why this matters:** a very large issue level can support continuing pressure on policy without creating a fresh election surprise. Conversely, a fast-rising attitude may not yet be a voting priority. Kalshi price changes should be compared with the appropriate dimension, rather than a composite “salience score” that mixes these distinct objects.

## 3. Three election findings with direct portfolio implications

### Data centers: a policy constraint can become broader than the partisan contest

Wisconsin's August survey found costs outweigh benefits among 64% of Republicans, 80% of independents and 94% of Democrats; 67% favored at least a one-year construction ban. Yet only 5% chose data centers as their most important current issue, versus 43% for inflation. Crowley led Tiffany 49–44 among likely voters but tied 44–44 among registered voters. That turnout-screen difference is substantive; it does not demonstrate that data centers caused the candidate gap. [Marquette Wisconsin](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2026/08/26/marquette-law-poll-finds-crowley-leading-tiffany-among-likely-voters-tied-among-all-registered-voters/).

The policy translation is already visible through different institutions:

| Jurisdiction and date | Verified stage | What can be concluded |
|---|---|---|
| Ohio, July 9, 2025 | PUCO ordered a separate AEP Ohio data-center tariff | Customer cost protection predates the current campaign |
| Maine, April 29, 2026 | Moratorium veto sustained; advisory council created | A passed bill did not become a statewide ban |
| Colorado, May 14, 2026 | SB26-189 enacted; key obligations start January 2027 | AI rules can create 2027 exposure under an existing state law |
| Pennsylvania, August 18, 2026 | Executive Order 2026-05 | Permitting and Fast Track eligibility tightened; utility-cost protections to be advocated at the PUC |
| Federal, March 20, 2026 | White House legislative framework | Uniform AI policy is sought; this release does not establish enacted preemption |

Sources: [PUCO order notice](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/OHPUC/bulletins/3e8bb79), [Maine executive action and veto outcome](https://www.maine.gov/governor/mills/news/governor-mills-signs-executive-order-establish-maine-data-center-advisory-council-2026-04-29), [Colorado enacted bill](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189), [Pennsylvania signed order](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/governor/documents/eo2026_05_protecting%20pennsylvania%20consumers%20from%20data%20center%20impacts_final_executed.pdf), [White House framework](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/03/president-donald-j-trump-unveils-national-ai-legislative-framework/).

**Investment inference:** the useful distinction is increasingly between projects with credible cost allocation/local approval and projects whose economics assume other customers absorb infrastructure costs. Election outcomes may change the intensity and mechanism of this constraint without removing it. For long-only selection, require project-level proof of approvals, funding responsibility and customer commitments before capitalizing distant AI-related growth. Treat suppliers' exposure to delayed construction separately from owners' exposure to higher operating costs. This is a research conclusion about risk selection, not evidence those differences are already mispriced.

### Affordability: voters agree on the problem more than on who should pay

In Pew's open-ended July election question, economy-related responses accounted for 29% of registered voters, including 15% naming cost of living/affordability. Those categories overlap. KFF's June fieldwork found 51% of voters rated healthcare costs extremely important for candidates to discuss, while 55% of Republican voters gave that rating to government-health-program fraud. These instruments measure different priorities; they should not be averaged. [Pew election agenda](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/07/23/as-the-2026-midterms-approach-economy-is-front-and-center/), [KFF July 16 release](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/poll-costs-are-the-top-health-care-issue-for-voters-in-the-midterms-but-fraud-tops-republicans-list-as-trump-administration-pushes-crackdown/).

CMS's June rule provides a concrete near-term transmission path: community-engagement conditions for applicable Medicaid populations begin in January 2027 unless a permitted exception/delay applies, while state-dependent outreach can occur July–September 2026. Existing beneficiaries' review timing depends on renewal initiation. A new governor cannot simply erase the federal requirement. [CMS interim final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/03/2026-11094/medicaid-program-community-engagement-requirement-for-certain-individuals).

**Investment inference:** classify relief proposals by incidence—taxpayer-funded transfer, regulated price, corporate margin concession, or supply expansion—and time to household benefit. For a 12–24 month portfolio, prioritize businesses whose cash flow survives several plausible ways of paying for relief. A subsidy can support demand but increase financing pressure; a capped price can help reported inflation while reducing investment or service availability. Exposure to payer rules, volume and reimbursement should be separated rather than applying a single “healthcare winner” label.

Housing also needs this distinction: the March housing executive order directs federal regulatory review; it does not by itself deliver housing units or override every local approval constraint. [White House housing action](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/03/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-removes-regulatory-barriers-to-affordable-home-construction/).

### Gasoline and Iran: the election channel and the Fed channel can point differently

Gallup's April family-finance survey found energy named by 13%, versus 3% a year earlier. Its July confidence index nevertheless recovered to −31 from −45 in May. These observations can coexist: affordability pressure is large while the direction of sentiment changes with the fieldwork window. [Gallup personal finances](https://news.gallup.com/poll/708905/affordability-dominates-americans-financial-worries.aspx), [Gallup July confidence](https://news.gallup.com/poll/713018/economic-confidence-improves-july.aspx).

The August EIA outlook assumed severe Hormuz constraints through August and gradual recovery beginning in September, projecting Q3 Brent around $85, $11 above its July forecast. This is an explicit assumption, not the probability of diplomatic success. On July 29 the FOMC held 3.50–3.75%, with three dissenters preferring a hike; on September 4 BLS reported August payroll growth of 162,000 and unemployment of 4.1%. The frozen evidence does not justify assuming affordability pressure brings immediate Fed easing. [Archived EIA August outlook](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/aug26.pdf), [July FOMC decision](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a.htm), [September 4 BLS vintage](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm).

**Investment inference:** distinguish oil relief from restored supply, relief from weaker demand, and persistent oil pressure alongside resilient employment. Each can alter incumbent approval and equity cash flows differently. A long-only hedge against household cost pressure can fail if it assumes lower oil necessarily signals stronger consumption, or if it assumes a political setback forces lower policy rates. Track energy-input exposure, household demand and financing duration separately.

## 5. Joining the evidence to Kalshi and equity pricing

Use three separate ledgers before making an investment claim:

1. **Public and electoral relevance:** comparable poll levels/changes, jurisdiction, voter population, turnout screen and actual office. A new speech or market listing can motivate review but cannot substitute for this evidence.
2. **Policy execution:** exact proposal, responsible institution, current stage, legal authority, coalition hurdles, effective date and implementation discretion. The Maine veto and Pennsylvania order must not receive the same code.
3. **Market and company transmission:** date-matched liquid Kalshi prices, spreads, volume and open interest; equity relative performance and publicly documented revenues, costs, capital spending and financing exposure.

A defensible emerging-theme finding requires corroboration across these ledgers. A price move with no new comparable poll can be a reassessment, not new mass salience. More volume identifies trading activity, not whether investors are collectively net long: every matched contract has opposing exposure. Flat prices with rising concern can reflect prior pricing, weak election relevance, expected policy blockage or poor liquidity. Distinguish those explanations before calling a mispricing.

For state-national comparisons, use stable cohorts of the same contract families and maturity horizons. Condition mention markets on the speaker, event and available word set. Do not infer regional opinion from a market's name or from trading geography. Report missing state polls and absent policy contracts explicitly.

For equity decisions, the current packet supports **due-diligence priorities and scenario distinctions**. It does not yet estimate policy-specific expected returns, investor crowding, or causal vote effects. The main report should combine these facts with its measured Kalshi and equity results before selecting overweight/underweight positions.

## Focused checks for the market-results interpretation

**Wisconsin primary confound.** The county clerk's official notice dated April 9 confirms the statewide partisan primary was August 11. A price observation at 15:07 UTC that day corresponds to 10:07 a.m. CDT on primary day. Marquette identifies August 12–20 as its first post-primary survey. Thus an August 11–September 8 gubernatorial price change spans nomination resolution, a defined candidate matchup and post-primary polling. A contemporaneously unchanged national House probability can suggest state-specific information, but cannot identify an effect of data-center concern. **Pennsylvania's exact mechanism.** For new applications involving data centers above 25 MW, GRID consent-agreement participants receive rolling permit review, but issuance waits for local approvals. Nonparticipants face additional local/water-approval prerequisites and cannot receive rolling permit issuance. The order also removes data centers from Fast Track and directs updates to exemption-program procedures. Its Section 3 instructs counsel to advocate for PUC tariffs allocating interconnection and reliability-backstop costs to data centers; it does not itself establish those tariffs. [Signed Executive Order 2026-05, sections 1 and 3](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/governor/documents/eo2026_05_protecting%20pennsylvania%20consumers%20from%20data%20center%20impacts_final_executed.pdf).

**Joint federal-control scenarios.** Hold the Republican presidency fixed through the ordinary four-year term. The following are institutional implications, not probabilities or guaranteed policy programs.

| House / Senate | Resulting constraint |
|---|---|
| Republican / Republican | Most direct route for Republican fiscal legislation, conditional on margins, cohesion and the applicable Senate procedure |
| Democratic / Republican | House bargaining and oversight change; Republican Senate nomination control remains |
| Republican / Democratic | Democratic Senate can impede nominations and reshape legislation; Republican House remains a separate fiscal gatekeeper |
| Democratic / Democratic | Both legislative agendas and oversight change; major reversals still face presidential presentment and veto constraints |

Presidential tenure, veto/presentment and Senate consent follow distinct constitutional provisions. A 50–50 Senate under a continuing Republican vice president normally gives Republicans a floor tie-breaking advantage; it does not mechanically assign every committee arrangement or discharge procedure. The 2021 Senate adopted a specific procedural resolution and separate committee-membership resolutions. For market coding, apply the contract's definition of control and keep policy capacity separate.
