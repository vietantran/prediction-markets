# Electoral evidence, diffusion and implementation

Study freeze: **September 8, 2026, 15:07 UTC**. Market quotations are frozen observations, not current executable prices. The public packet contains 29 selected records: 27 dated records and two standing institutional sources. It is a purposive evidence packet, not an exhaustive census of documents, states, policies or surveys. The additional Federal Reserve institutional FAQ is standing background, not a campaign event.

The programme keeps five different concepts separate: prompted concern, stated electoral or current-issue priority, policy preference, trading activity, and implemented policy. A rise in one does not automatically establish a rise in another. All tables below are produced from `inputs/public_evidence.json` or the separately frozen market snapshots. Survey questions are paraphrased. Earlier-wave sample/design details are incomplete, so percentage-point changes are descriptive rather than formal significance claims.

The investment inference is a change in the composition of household pressure, particularly gasoline, rather than a proven uniform rise in every affordability issue. This matters because relief through lower input prices, subsidies, regulated prices and tax changes has different beneficiaries and funding costs. Persistent healthcare concern can still be economically material without being a new political trend.

## What is becoming national or crossing party lines?

The clearest comparable finding is data-center cost-benefit opposition:

| Population | Republican | Independent | Democratic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| National adults, July | 66% | 72% | 80% |
| Wisconsin registered voters, August | 64% | 80% | 94% |

These are negative assessments of the same cost-benefit proposition within each survey, not support for a uniform national ban. Party subgroups have greater sampling uncertainty. National opposition already exists; this evidence does not identify Wisconsin as the cause or origin of its national spread. The selected state cases cannot estimate a national diffusion rate. [National survey](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2026/08/05/new-marquette-law-school-poll-national-survey-finds-inflation-and-cost-of-living-the-most-important-issue-economy-next-most-important/), [Wisconsin survey](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2026/08/26/marquette-law-poll-finds-crowley-leading-tiffany-among-likely-voters-tied-among-all-registered-voters/).

In Wisconsin, 67% favor at least a one-year data-center ban, but only 5% choose data centers as their most important current issue, compared with 43% choosing inflation. Crowley leads Tiffany 49–44 among likely voters but the race is 44–44 among all registered voters. That turnout-screen difference and the August 11 primary mean a race-probability move cannot be assigned to data-center attitudes. The public-opinion result supports examining durable political pressure, while withholding a claim of issue-driven vote switching. [Wisconsin survey](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2026/08/26/marquette-law-poll-finds-crowley-leading-tiffany-among-likely-voters-tied-among-all-registered-voters/).

Healthcare illustrates the distinction between a shared concern and a shared remedy: KFF finds costs important to voters, while fraud is particularly important to Republicans. The source contains one comparable wave in this packet. It does not establish either accelerating salience or agreement on financing, enforcement or coverage policy. [KFF healthcare priorities](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/poll-costs-are-the-top-health-care-issue-for-voters-in-the-midterms-but-fraud-tops-republicans-list-as-trump-administration-pushes-crackdown/).

## State policy has several economically different forms

| State | Verified response | What it does not establish | Underwriting implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wisconsin | Survey support for a moratorium; election approaching | An enacted statewide ban | Examine prospective authority, projects and local approvals |
| Ohio | PUCO data-center tariff order | A current uniform statewide construction ban | Customer commitments and cost allocation may enable investment while increasing customer obligations |
| Pennsylvania | Permit sequencing, GRID participation and local-approval conditions; tariff advocacy | Universal construction prohibition or automatic PUC tariff approval | Examine project delays, financing and customer cost recovery separately |
| Maine | Sustained veto of moratorium bill; advisory council | A moratorium in force | Exemptions and local jobs/tax revenue can change the remedy |
| Colorado | Enacted automated-decision law; major duties from January 2027 | Regulation of all AI revenue or a construction moratorium | Map covered applications and compliance obligations |

Ohio is a particularly useful counterexample to a simple anti-data-center thesis. Its July 2025 order sought to prevent cost shifting to other customers and also called for lifting AEP Ohio's connection moratorium. Protecting ratepayers can therefore support future connections. The evidence applies to this tariff process, not identical terms at every Ohio utility. [PUCO order announcement](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/OHPUC/bulletins/3e8bb79).

Pennsylvania's August 18 order distinguishes new applications above 25 MW by participation in GRID agreements, makes permit issuance conditional on local approvals and removes data centers from Fast Track. Advocacy for utility tariffs is a separate process. These distinctions point to timing, capital cost and allocation of infrastructure expense, rather than cancellation of every project. [Executed Pennsylvania order](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/governor/documents/eo2026_05_protecting%20pennsylvania%20consumers%20from%20data%20center%20impacts_final_executed.pdf).

Maine's governor supported a temporary moratorium with a specific project exemption but vetoed the bill presented to her; the legislature sustained that veto. The advisory council's recommendations are due January 29, 2027. Economic-development benefits and project design can consequently shape the policy even within an apparently restrictive debate. [Maine governor's announcement](https://www.maine.gov/governor/mills/news/governor-mills-signs-executive-order-establish-maine-data-center-advisory-council-2026-04-29).

Colorado's enacted framework covers consequential automated decisions, including some employment, housing, lending, insurance and healthcare applications. Developer documentation, notices, records and human review have different costs from restrictions on physical AI infrastructure. The recorded 34–1 Senate and 57–6 House votes are large majorities, but this packet does not infer party labels from vote totals. [Colorado SB26-189](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189).

The portfolio inference is that a federal-election hedge can leave substantial state and regulatory exposure untouched. The most useful company distinction may be existing versus prospective assets, or recoverable versus unrecoverable customer costs, rather than a generic pro-AI versus anti-AI classification. This is a mechanism to underwrite, not an estimated causal return.

Under the convention that the incumbent presidency continues, Democratic control of one or both chambers changes oversight and legislative bargaining but does not replace the president. Presentment, veto and Senate procedure remain relevant. Republican control of both chambers also does not guarantee every proposed law: internal agreement and procedure matter. Deadlines are equally important. Contracts about actions completed before January 2027 do not directly price legislation enacted by the incoming Congress. The new Wisconsin governor's term begins January 2027, while Pennsylvania's cited order already operates before the election. [Wisconsin official election notice](https://www.bayfieldcounty.wi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/20824/Notice-of-Election-Partisan-Primary-August-11-2026-and-General-Election-November-3-2026).

Finally, electoral affordability pressure does not directly instruct the FOMC. Monetary decisions do not require presidential or congressional approval. Fiscal policy, appointments, energy prices, employment and inflation are separate transmission channels. A strong Democratic House probability and a high probability of persistently elevated rates are not inherently inconsistent. ## Fundamental scenario methods and interpretation

`fundamentals.run()` computes 42 company cases: seven companies, three explicit assumptions and one- and two-year horizons. Each uses an eligible TTM EPS observation and the last completed-session raw close. Stale, missing or post-freeze facts are withheld. Microsoft's starting EPS removes the disclosed $0.67 annual OpenAI investment gain; the other companies retain reconstructed GAAP EPS. This is not a complete earnings-normalization exercise.

Terminal price is starting EPS × (1 + EPS CAGR)^years × exit P/E. Results are **price returns only**: dividends, tax and transaction costs are excluded. The lower, central and upper assumptions are designed financial sensitivities, not consensus forecasts, probabilities, statistically calibrated scenarios or election-conditioned earnings estimates. The labels do not assign likelihood. Cases are specified in `config/fundamental_cases.json`, together with dated company evidence, the operating mechanism, assumption rationale, decision gate and invalidation condition.

The programme also solves the EPS growth needed to preserve the share price, the growth needed for a 10% annual price-return hurdle, and the maximum entry price consistent with that hurdle. These are useful underwriting questions, not automatic price targets. A negative required entry discount means the selected case already exceeds the hurdle at the observed price.

The margin calculation is deliberately separate: a 100 bp change in consolidated EBIT margin at constant revenue equals 1% of sales. It is not a 100 bp change in a medical cost ratio, tariff, tax rate or EPS. Industrial CFO-minus-cash-capex is shown as a cash-flow proxy, not normalized distributable cash flow; it excludes lease additions. JPMorgan has no industrial FCF or EBIT-margin estimate. AEP remains excluded from valuation because the frozen selected current financial facts were withheld as stale.

These controls make the outputs auditable while preserving the PM's judgment about cash flows, policy feasibility and acceptable valuation.
