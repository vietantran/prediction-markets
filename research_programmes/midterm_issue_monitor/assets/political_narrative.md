## The electoral agenda: affordability is broad; the strongest increases are narrower

This analysis is frozen at **September 8, 2026, 15:07 UTC**. Its central political finding is a distinction between the level of concern and its direction of change. Healthcare and housing remain important, but gasoline costs and opposition to data-center development show the clearest increases in the comparable public series. Kalshi activity helps identify where uncertainty attracts trading; it does not establish which issue changes a person's vote.

Pew's July electoral question puts the economy first: 29% of registered voters named an economic issue they most wanted congressional candidates to discuss. Cost of living accounted for 15%, within that total. This identifies the agenda's centre of gravity, but the available cross-section does not establish a trend in electoral priority. [Pew's midterm agenda survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/07/23/as-the-2026-midterms-approach-economy-is-front-and-center/).

On comparable January–July questions, the share very concerned about gasoline rose from 34% to 56%. Electricity moved from 51% to 54%, housing from 62% to 64%, and healthcare from 71% to 69%. The gasoline/electricity comparisons use the matching separate-question half-samples. These are nominal percentage-point movements, without a formal significance calculation from incomplete prior-wave design information. [Pew's economic evaluations](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/07/23/americans-evaluations-of-the-economy-remain-negative/).

There is counterevidence to a blanket acceleration narrative: Gallup's share naming inflation as the country's most important problem fell from 15% in May to 11% in July. Personal cost concern, national problem priority and electoral voting motivation are different measurements. The portfolio question is consequently which costs are worsening, which remedies are feasible, and who pays for relief. [Gallup's July release](https://news.gallup.com/poll/713018/economic-confidence-improves-july.aspx).

## Election probabilities describe power, not a completed policy programme

The November 3 contest changes several institutions simultaneously. The dated Senate inventory has 53 Republicans, 45 Democrats and two independents caucusing with Democrats; its 2026 list includes 13 Democratic and 22 Republican-held seats, including special elections. State legislative races cover 6,139 seats in 46 states and 88 chambers. This breadth is why a House-control probability cannot represent the entire political exposure. ## Data-center opposition crosses parties; the remedy remains contested

{{cross_party_chart}}

Nationally, 73% say data-center costs exceed benefits, up from 62% in January. The latest subgroup readings are 66% among Republicans, 72% among independents and 80% among Democrats. This supports a concern that can persist across electoral outcomes. It does not demonstrate a shared preference for a national ban. [Marquette's national survey](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2026/08/05/new-marquette-law-school-poll-national-survey-finds-inflation-and-cost-of-living-the-most-important-issue-economy-next-most-important/).

Wisconsin's corresponding subgroup readings are 64%, 80% and 94%. Yet data centers are only 5% of respondents' most important current issue, against inflation's 43%. Overall support for at least a one-year ban is 67%; the packet does not establish party-specific agreement on that remedy. Broad opposition can create policy pressure without becoming the dominant vote-choice issue. [Wisconsin survey](https://law.marquette.edu/poll/2026/08/26/marquette-law-poll-finds-crowley-leading-tiffany-among-likely-voters-tied-among-all-registered-voters/).

## Five state cases show why implementation matters more than a generic backlash label

{{state_table}}

**Ohio** is the most useful counterexample to equating ratepayer protection with less development. PUCO's July 2025 tariff order addressed cost shifting and also called for lifting AEP Ohio's connection moratorium. Stronger customer obligations can protect utility investment while raising the customer's project hurdle. [PUCO announcement](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/OHPUC/bulletins/3e8bb79).

**Pennsylvania** instead changed permit sequencing and GRID participation conditions for new applications above 25 MW, removed data centers from Fast Track and directed tariff advocacy. That is neither a universal prohibition nor automatic approval of new utility tariffs. [Executed order](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/governor/documents/eo2026_05_protecting%20pennsylvania%20consumers%20from%20data%20center%20impacts_final_executed.pdf).

**Maine** sustained a moratorium-bill veto and established an advisory council. The governor's support for a temporary moratorium with a project exemption shows how local employment and tax benefits alter policy design. Recommendations are due January 29, 2027. **Wisconsin**, meanwhile, supplies opinion and election evidence, not an enacted ban in this packet. [Maine announcement](https://www.maine.gov/governor/mills/news/governor-mills-signs-executive-order-establish-maine-data-center-advisory-council-2026-04-29).

**Colorado** has an enacted automated-decision framework, with major duties beginning January 2027. Covered employment, housing, lending and healthcare applications face documentation and review requirements; this is a different exposure from physical construction restrictions. [Colorado law](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189). These five selected cases establish distinct mechanisms, not a nationwide moratorium count or measured diffusion rate.

## The investment horizon extends beyond contract deadlines

Many highlighted policy contracts end before the incoming Congress can legislate. Their odds cannot be treated as probabilities of a 2027–2028 congressional programme. Existing regulators, incumbent executives and already-enacted laws can matter sooner; new state officeholders inherit different powers and obligations.

Fed decisions require a further distinction. Monetary policy does not require presidential or congressional approval. Elections can affect fiscal choices and appointments, while energy prices, growth and inflation affect the economic environment. A high Democratic House probability can coexist with persistent-rate risk without contradiction. [Federal Reserve independence](https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/about_12799.htm). For equities, the practical task is to separate development timing, recoverable costs, financing and demand—then test valuation under each mechanism rather than assigning one return forecast to a party label.
